3. Constitution of Nepal – Part 2: Citizenship (Articles 10–15)
Article 10 – No deprivation of citizenship
No Nepali citizen shall be deprived of the right to obtain citizenship. Nepal provides for a single federal citizenship with provincial identity.
Article 11 – Who is a citizen of Nepal
Sets out citizenship status at commencement of the Constitution, citizenship by descent, citizenship for children of citizens, naturalized citizenship and related constitutional categories.
Article 12 – Citizenship with gender identity
A person acquiring citizenship by descent may obtain a Nepali citizenship certificate with gender identity through the name of the mother or father.
Article 13 – Acquisition, reacquisition and termination
Other arrangements concerning acquisition, reacquisition and termination of citizenship are to be provided by federal law.
Article 14 – Non-resident Nepali citizenship
A person who is a citizen of a foreign country outside SAARC, and who previously had Nepali citizenship by descent or birth, or whose parent/grandparent was such a Nepali citizen, may receive NRN citizenship with economic, social and cultural rights as provided by federal law.
Article 15 – Other citizenship provisions
Records identifying every Nepali citizen and other citizenship arrangements are to be provided by federal law.
4. Nepal Citizenship Act, 2063 – Core Knowledge Map
5. Second Amendment Act, 2082 – Verified Changes
The official Ministry of Law PDF identifies this as the Nepal Citizenship (Second Amendment) Act, 2082, Act No. 08 of 2082, authenticated on 2082/06/05. It states that it comes into force immediately.
1. Section 3(4A) added
Where one parent acquired citizenship by birth and died or became out of contact before obtaining the other parent's Nepali citizenship, the special route is tied to Section 5(5A).
2. Section 5(5A) added
A person born in Nepal to a Nepali-citizen mother and residing in Nepal may qualify where the father is out of contact/identity cannot be established, or the mother cannot identify the father, or documents mention a father but his identity cannot be established, subject to the statutory self-declaration mechanism.
3. Section 5(3)(c) amended
The wording changes the self-declaration requirement to involve both the applicant and the applicant's mother; an exception applies where the mother has died or is medically certified as unable to understand/act.
4. Section 5(5A)/(5B) additions
Creates naturalized-citizenship routes for specified cases, including a person under Section 3(4A), and a person born abroad to a Nepali mother who permanently resides in Nepal, has not obtained foreign citizenship/passport, and whose father is unidentified, subject to prescribed self-declaration.
5. Section 8A(1A) added
If a person does not want the name, surname or address of either parent shown, the citizenship certificate must omit the requested parent's details.
6. Section 18 replaced
A person aggrieved by an order/decision under the Act may apply within 35 days through the concerned DAO or directly to the Secretary of the Ministry of Home Affairs. The amendment specifies reporting and decision time limits and makes the Secretary's decision/order final under the statutory review mechanism, except for Government of Nepal orders/decisions.
7. Section 21(3A) updated
The penalty provision is extended to the newly added Section 5(5A) and 5(5B) situations.
8. Section 22A added
A minor identity card may be issued in the prescribed form by the authority under Section 20 where one parent is a Nepali citizen or in the specified Section 8(1)(c) case.
Important: The above is a structured explanation of the verified amendment text. For legal interpretation, the original authenticated PDF should be retrieved with the answer.

7. DAO Kathmandu Citizen Charter – Practical Administrative Layer
The Kathmandu DAO Citizen Charter is an administrative service document. It contains service details, required documents, processing time, fees, responsible section/officer and complaint officer. It should be treated as practical administrative guidance rather than the controlling legal authority.
Descent-based citizenship: local-level recommendation using Schedule 1; citizenship documents of parents/eligible relatives; identification/verification; birth/education documents and other evidence depending on the case.
Marital naturalized citizenship: Schedule 7, spouse's citizenship, marriage registration, evidence of initiating foreign citizenship renunciation, spouse/relative identification and required photographs/visa evidence where applicable.
Citizenship duplicate/copy: Schedule 2 and proof of citizenship number/issue date; additional migration/record-verification documents may be required for citizenship issued by another district.
Administrative timing and fees shown in the charter can change; the chatbot should retrieve the latest DAO page before answering a question about current service time or fee.
8. Other DAO Cross-Check

Kaski DAO: citizen charter lists citizenship by descent, cases involving an unidentified father, NRN citizenship and duplicate citizenship services, with local procedural/document details.
9. Non-Resident Nepali (NRN) Citizenship
Constitution Article 14 provides the constitutional basis. The 2079 amendment introduced/expanded statutory NRN citizenship provisions, and MoHA publishes Schedule forms for the NRN citizenship certificate.
MoHA publishes Schedule 8G: application for NRN citizenship certificate.
MoHA publishes Schedule 8D: commitment/undertaking.
MoHA publishes Schedule 8Cha: oath.
DAO Kaski's charter also lists NRN citizenship and requires proof connecting the applicant or qualifying parent/grandparent to Nepali citizenship, together with the prescribed local recommendation.
Production rule: Never answer 'NRN citizenship gives full political rights' unless the exact legal source supports it. Article 14 expressly describes economic, social and cultural rights under federal law.
